# Apple Nutritional Profile and Health-Based Mapping

**Basis:** Fresh Pink Lady® apple, including skin and excluding core. Values are per 100 g as reported by the commercial source.

## 1. Nutritional Profile

| Nutrient / Feature | Value per 100 g |
|---|---:|
| Energy | 51 kcal |
| Fat | 0.5 g |
| Saturated fat | 0.1 g |
| Carbohydrate | 11.6 g |
| Total sugars | 11.6 g |
| Protein | 0.6 g |
| Dietary fibre | 1.8 g |
| Salt | 0 g |
| Vitamin C | 6 mg |
| Potassium | 100 mg |
| Iron | 0.1 mg |
| Calcium | 5 mg |

**Commercial source:** https://www.pinkladyapples.co.uk/the-pink-lady-story/apple-nutrition-vitamins-and-minerals

## 2. Final Health-Condition Mapping

| Health condition | Result | Main nutritional factors | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diabetes / blood-glucose management | **Favorable** | Carbohydrate, total sugars, dietary fibre, energy | Whole apple provides carbohydrate and natural sugars together with fibre and relatively low energy density. The American Diabetes Association emphasizes whole fruits over juice because whole fruit retains fibre. |
| Hypertension / high blood pressure | **Favorable** | Salt, potassium | The commercial profile reports 0 g salt and 100 mg potassium per 100 g. The American Heart Association identifies lower sodium intake and adequate dietary potassium as dietary factors that can support blood-pressure management. |
| Iron-deficiency anemia | **Less Favorable** | Iron, vitamin C | At 0.1 mg iron per 100 g, apple is not a substantial iron source. Its vitamin C can help absorption of non-heme iron from other foods, but the apple itself should not be treated as an iron-rich food. |
| Constipation / inadequate fibre intake | **Favorable** | Dietary fibre | The apple provides 1.8 g dietary fibre per 100 g, and the commercial source notes that much of the fibre is in the skin. NIDDK specifically lists apples with the skin among fruit sources of fibre. |

## 3. Interpretation Note

These results are intended for the Fruitelligence rule-based recommendation framework. “Favorable” means that the selected nutritional factors support a positive classification under the project's decision-support logic; it does not mean that the food treats or prevents a medical condition. Individual dietary needs and medical conditions should be assessed by a qualified professional.
